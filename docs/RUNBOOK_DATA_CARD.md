# Authored demonstration runbooks: data card

The frozen corpus under `data/runbooks/demo` has 24 authored demonstration documents: 18 generic runbooks published on 2025-12-01 and six historical notes published on 2026-02-01. The manifest SHA-256 is `1930189c68b7fd64b72862ab93c37744d1297fba270a9671cdafd0e551043497`; each source file has an individual hash and stable source ID in that manifest. The author is the TraceCause project; the documents are labeled `authored_demo`, licensed Apache-2.0 and contain no external text. The generator is `scripts/generate_runbook_corpus.py`; a change to any document requires a new document version and manifest hash before ingestion.

The runbooks cover the five MiniShop service roles, generic HTTP/trace diagnostics, alias examples, deliberately conflicting older guidance and an irrelevant maintenance reminder. The six notes explicitly report earlier demo interpretations; they are published after the synthetic smoke episode and must not be eligible at its January 2026 cutoff. They do not identify or solve a specific test episode. The corpus is short, synthetic and educational; it is not production guidance or a measured incident archive.

`scripts/scan_runbook_leakage.py` checks source hashes and scans the corpus against private synthetic episode IDs, source/semantic hashes, seeds and intervention timestamps in the offline gold sidecar. It also flags private-label field names and UUIDs. The completed scan covered 24 documents and 121 private truth files with zero findings. This scan cannot establish semantic non-leakage by itself. The authored text was inspected for test-target statements; independent human review remains pending before external release.

`data/runbooks/annotations.json` contains 12 authored mention-resolution cases covering curated aliases, overlapping labels, fuzzy near-misses and different environments. All 12 expected outcomes passed in the unit test; nine produce a reviewable link status and three correctly produce no link. This is a small self-authored validation set, not an independently labeled precision benchmark. An offline corpus review at `reports/runbooks/link_review.json` reported 66 accepted mentions, zero ambiguous and two unresolved fuzzy mentions in the single demo environment. The report is generated and ignored by Git; rerun `scripts/report_runbook_links.py` to reproduce it.

The pinned MiniLM tokenizer revision is `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`. Its committed `tokenizer.json` SHA-256 is `be50c3628f2bf5bb5e3a7f17b1f74611b2561a3a27eeab05e5aa30f411572037`; `sentence_bert_config.json` SHA-256 is `fc1993fde0a95c24ec6c022539d41cf6e2f7c9721e5415d6fb6897472a9cd4b7`. Chunking disables tokenizer-file padding/truncation, measures title plus heading plus excerpt, targets at most 180 word pieces and enforces the model's 256-word-piece limit. This phase downloads tokenizer metadata only, not encoder weights. No embedding quality, entity-link accuracy beyond the authored examples, or causal accuracy is claimed.
